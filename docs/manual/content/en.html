Welcome

Scripture Alone is a Bible for studying God's word without anything in the way: no ads, no trackers, no account and no server holding your data.

The whole Bible lives on your deviceiPhoneiPadMacdevice, so the app reads offline, anywhere, from the moment it installs. The New American Standard Bible — NASB 2020 is built in and is what the app opens to. Everything else you add — other Bibles, commentary, the original languages — downloads once and then works offline too.

Your highlights, notes and favorites sync between your own devices through your private iCloud. Nobody else, including the developer, can see them.

Importing a Bible you own

Reading a whole Bible takes a minute or two — longer for a study Bible or a PDF. When it's done, a summary shows what came in: every book, any verses the translation leaves out because the oldest manuscripts don't have them (such as Acts 8:37), and any real gaps. A file that reads too poorly is refused rather than half-imported.

  • Study Bibles bring their notes, book introductions, essays and maps; the notes appear in Study's Commentary tab.
  • The words of Christ carry over in red.
  • Your imports reach your other devices through your own iCloud, and your Apple Watch when the translation's terms allow it. They are never shared with anyone else.
  • Your imports stay on this device, and reach your Wear OS watch when the translation's terms allow it. They are never shared with anyone else.
  • Copy-protected (DRM) files are refused. The app contains no code to unlock them.

Online translations

The ESV, CSB and NKJV can be read inside Scripture Alone over the internet, using a free key from the publisher's own service. It takes about five minutes, once.

Why a key of your own? The free tiers are personal allowances. A key shipped inside the app would be used up by strangers within a day, and it would break the terms these services are offered on. Your key is stored in your device's keychain and syncs through iCloud Keychain to your other devices, end-to-end encrypted.Your key is stored encrypted on your device, and Google's Block Store backs it up, end-to-end encrypted, so it comes back on a new device set up from this one.

Good to know
  • Online translations need a connection. What you read is kept on the device up to the publisher's limit, and they can't be searched offline.
  • Your picks and keys come back by themselves on a new device or after reinstalling, through iCloud.

Keepsakes, family and export

Your marked Bible is worth handing down. Open Aa › Keepsake & Export.

A Keepsake Bible

  1. TapClick Create a Keepsake. Give it a name and a dedication ("For Sarah, from Dad"), and choose the translation it opens in.
  2. Add a passphrase if you'd like it protected.
  3. Give the file to your family: AirDropQuick Share, email, Messages, a USB drive, or kept with your will.

Family open it with Open a Keepsake File… and read your Bible as you marked it — read-only, kept apart from their own notes. The format is an open ZIP of JSON, so it stays readable for generations. A keepsake opens in Scripture Alone on Android and iPhone alike.

Share with Family

Invite family members to see your highlights, notes and favorites live, read-only, as you keep reading. TapClick Start Sharing and Invite… and send the invitation. They see "Reading Dad's Bible · updated 5 min. ago", and can read it offline. Stop Sharing any time; they keep the last copy. It happens entirely between your iCloud and theirs.

In Notes, the Export menu saves all your notes, just the ones you've filtered, or a single note, as a typeset PDF, Markdown (one file or a folder) or plain text, with the verses quoted in your translation.

Questions and answers

Does it work without internet?

Yes. The NASB 2020 is built in, and anything you download or import stays available offline. Only online translations (chapter 5) need a connection.

A Bible is still downloading. Can I read?

Yes — read the NASB 2020 while it finishes. If a download fails, a banner offers Try Again.

Why isn't the ESV, CSB or NKJV just included?

Those translations belong to their publishers, and including them takes a license. We've asked for one. Meanwhile, read them with your own key (chapter 5) or import a copy you own (chapter 4).

My notes aren't on my other device.

Check that both devices are signed in to the same Apple Account with iCloud turned on for Scripture Alone, and give them a minute on Wi-Fi.

Will my notes come to a new device?

Yes, through Android's backup, when you set up the new device from your old one or from a backup of it — as long as your library fits in the backup. Make sure backup is turned on in your device's Settings. Scripture Alone doesn't sync between two devices in use.

An import says it has gaps.

Some translations genuinely leave out verses, and some files are simply incomplete. You can read whatever imported either way; a cleaner edition of the file usually fixes it.

Is it really free?

Yes: no purchases, no subscriptions, no ads. It was built to help people study God's word and grow closer to Christ.

Where can I get help?

Visit wemiller.com/apps/scripture-alone, or use Support under Aa.
